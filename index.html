About

I live in Seattle and run my own company, Hunter Technologies, where I build commercial software applications. Occasionally, I take on high-impact consulting projects with startups: short term, surgical strikes solving difficult technical problems. I'm well versed in LLM-based coding assistants, CLIs and IDEs, and I lean on agentic coding to amplify my impact so I can focus on the harder things: solving the right problem, getting people on the same page and mentoring junior engineers.

Prior to this, I worked at Amazon Web Services (AWS) as a Software Engineer where I focused on payments and security, building web-based distributed & embedded systems. I designed and built many services that scaled cleanly and predictably, and were extensible and maintainable for other engineers. In my work, I collaborate closely with product and business teams, helping translate vague requirements into clear technical solutions so we build the right thing, not just the requested thing.

A niche specialty I picked up almost by accident: JavaCard applets and smart-card standards (GlobalPlatform, EMVCo, ISO 7816/14443, Mifare) for identity, transit and payments.

My roots are in Istanbul, Turkey, where I was born and raised, and where I attended Istanbul Erkek. I did my undergrad in Computer Science at Emory University in Atlanta.

Outside of work, I like tinkering with self-hosting and infrastructure. I'm a big Seattle sports fan. I like staying active, playing basketball, and exploring craft beers.
